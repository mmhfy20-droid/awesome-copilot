# Product Design and Discovery

## Purpose
Translate a business goal into a product that solves a real user problem. Do not jump directly into coding.

## Workflow
1. Identify the target users, primary problem, context of use, business outcome, and constraints.
2. Separate confirmed requirements from assumptions. Label assumptions and propose how to validate them.
3. Define the primary user journeys and edge cases.
4. Create an information architecture, screen inventory, and navigation model.
5. Prioritize an MVP using user value, risk, effort, and dependencies.
6. Define acceptance criteria for each MVP capability.
7. Review privacy, accessibility, localization, low-bandwidth, and device constraints.
8. Present the product brief and unresolved decisions before implementation.

## Deliverables
- Product brief and measurable success criteria
- User personas only when supported by evidence
- User journeys and screen inventory
- MVP scope and out-of-scope list
- Acceptance criteria and open questions

## Guardrails
- Do not invent customer research, market size, regulatory approval, or business results.
- Do not treat an attractive mockup as proof of usability.
- For health, pharmacy, finance, or other regulated domains, flag domain-specific review requirements.
- Ask one focused question only when a missing decision blocks meaningful progress.
